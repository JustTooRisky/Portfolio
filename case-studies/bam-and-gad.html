Before

What was there, and what was hard to trust

Reference: public site at bamandgadsolicitors.com.ng (captured Aug 2026). The firm is a commercial practice at 17/19 Allen Avenue, Ikeja, handling corporate, maritime, finance, real estate, and immigration. The team page lists five lawyers but gives almost nothing to verify them.

  1. Five photos, one biography. The team directory shows five lawyers; only Dr. Yemi Opemuti's page has any biographical text, and even that only appears on hover. The other four pages contain photos, social icons, and nothing more.
  2. Senior counsel hidden behind interaction. Dr. Opemuti's record (the firm's strongest credential) requires a hover-and-click to access. A senior counsel's authority should not be an easter egg.
  3. Empty individual pages. Clicking any other team-member's name lands on a page with a portrait, social icons, and zero biography — a trust failure for a referral-driven practice.
  4. No matter-type triage. The "Quick Contact" form has a free-text "Your message" field with no practice-area or urgency selector — high-stakes enquirers hesitate when the next step is ambiguous.
  5. Visual sameness. All pages share the same template, so a senior counsel's record reads as a blog post, not a credential.

Approach

I wanted to make authority legible

"I wanted to make the counsel's record clearer, reduce the firm's catalogue feel, and make the next step obvious without inventing a biography that isn't public."

The strategy was to invert the current information order: person first, firm second, catalogue last.

  • Open with a portrait, name, year called, courts, and a one-line authority claim — a legal client's first question answered above the fold.
  • Group practice areas into three plain-English clusters (Corporate & Commercial · Property, Finance & Maritime · Advisory) instead of eight isolated links.
  • Replace empty team-member pages with a structured credential block: matters, admissions, languages, contact — all verifiable, no invented wins.
  • Single low-friction CTA ("Request a consultation") with matter-type select that mirrors how the firm actually triages — corporate, property, maritime, immigration.
  • Editorial restraint: one green accent, generous white space, and a tighter type scale so authority reads as weight, not noise.

Implementation

How it was built

Responsive & calm

  • Two-column bio grid collapses to single column at 820px; portrait keeps 3:4 ratio
  • Fluid headings with clamp(), no breakpoint-specific font overrides
  • All images with explicit dimensions; no layout shift on load

Semantic & accessible

  • figure / figcaption for portrait, label / select pairing for matter type
  • Focus-visible on every CTA; tab order follows visual order
  • Text contrast checked (ink #1C1B18 on paper #F4F1EA, green #145C46 on paper)

Content honesty

  • Credentials sourced from the firm's public website and published profiles — no invented bios
  • Practice areas grouped from the firm's listed services (Admiralty, Banking, Business & Industry, Real Estate, Immigration)
  • Address and phone are the firm's public details (17/19 Allen Ave, +234 806 278 7594)

Reusable

  • Bio template works for any counsel: swap portrait, name, admissions, clusters
  • Uses same tokens (--paper / --ink / --green) as portfolio home — one CSS source

Lighthouse proof: placeholder until a real run on the deployed URL. Target 90+ on Performance and Accessibility.
