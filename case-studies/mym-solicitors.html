MYM Solicitors LLP: a UK firm homepage, rebuilt for clarity

Sixteen practice-area tiles, a buried fee schedule, and urgency fragmented across footers made the homepage ask too much at once. The redesign groups practice areas into four, surfaces fees honestly, and puts the emergency number where the call sits.

Before

What was there, and why it felt heavy

Reference: mymsolicitors.co.uk (captured Aug 2026). MYM is an SRA-regulated practice (SRA ID 8010908) with offices at 329–331 High Street, Slough SL1 1TX, plus Gerrards Cross and London. The site lists around 16 practice areas, from immigration and family to wills, probate, and employment, with walk-in and 24-hour emergency availability.

  1. Practice-area sprawl. 16+ areas rendered as a uniform tile grid, covering immigration, family, wills, and employment, without grouping or guidance; visitors scan but cannot prioritise.
  2. Fee disclosure buried. Consultation £100 + VAT and detailed immigration fees (£500–£2500) are present but deep on dense pages with inconsistent formatting; the trust signal is hidden by presentation.
  3. Urgency fragmented. “Emergency / 24 Hours: 0203 1377751” and “Walk-in: 09:00–17:30” appear in multiple footers/banners but lack a single, scannable availability block near the CTA.
  4. Performance cues. Heavy legacy tiles, repeated social-icon blocks, and unsized images create CLS risk; mobile requires excessive scrolling before any matter-type filter appears.
  5. Accessibility gaps. Low-contrast white-on-image tiles and icon-only social links without accessible names, fixable with WCAG 2.1 AA defaults.

Approach

I wanted to make clarity do the selling

“I wanted to make practice areas scannable, reduce the fee-hunt, and make urgency (walk-in and 24-hour help) obvious without changing what MYM actually offers.”

  • Cluster 16 areas into four plain-English groups: Immigration & Human Rights · Family & Islamic Family · Wills, Probate & Property · Employment & Civil, each with a one-line scope and a single entry CTA.
  • Surface fees honestly but cleanly: keep the £100 consultation and the published ranges, but set them in a legible rate table with VAT noted, not scattered paragraphs.
  • Single availability ribbon (Walk-in Mon–Fri 09:30–17:30 · Emergency 0203 1377751 · WhatsApp) pinned near the primary “Request a callback” action.
  • Editorial restraint and performance budget: no grid of 16 heavy tiles, no duplicate footers, just semantic lists, explicit image sizes, and one accent green on paper.
  • Insights section prepared for headless WordPress: posts fetched via /wp-json/wp/v2/posts so the firm can publish without touching markup.

Implementation

How it was built

Performance first

  • Replaced 16-tile image grid with 4 semantic cards, reducing DOM nodes and removing large tile images
  • Explicit width/height on all media, no CLS on load; minimal JS (only form status toggle)
  • Single font preconnect, no duplicate footers or social-icon repeats

Accessible defaults

  • White-on-green tiles removed; paper backgrounds with ink text meet 4.5:1
  • All icon links receive visible text equivalents; form labels explicitly associated
  • Focus states on every CTA; tab order matches card then availability then callback

Honest content

  • Fees quoted verbatim: £100 + VAT consultation, immigration ranges £500–£2500 as published
  • Offices listed as public: Slough SL1 1TX, Gerrards Cross, London W5 5SL; SRA ID 8010908 noted
  • No invented reviews: “417 reviews · 4.8” is the firm’s public count, not a new claim

Headless-ready

  • Insights section is API-shaped: expects WP REST /posts, renders title/excerpt/link
  • Graceful fallback if the API is unavailable: a static link with no broken layout
  • Content authors publish in WP; front-end remains hand-coded static
